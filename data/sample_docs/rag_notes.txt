RAG Architecture Notes

Retrieval-Augmented Generation combines a retrieval system with a generative
language model. Instead of relying purely on the model's parametric memory,
RAG fetches relevant text chunks from an external knowledge base at query
time and feeds them into the model's context window.

Why RAG matters: LLMs have a fixed knowledge cutoff and can hallucinate
facts. RAG grounds responses in retrievable, verifiable source documents,
and lets you update the knowledge base without retraining the model.

The core components are: a document loader, a chunking strategy, an
embedding model, a vector store, a retriever, and a generator. Each piece
can be swapped independently, which is why RAG systems are often built
with clean interfaces between stages.

Chunk size is a critical tuning parameter. Chunks that are too large dilute
the embedding's semantic focus, hurting retrieval precision. Chunks that
are too small lose surrounding context, hurting the generator's ability to
produce a coherent answer.
